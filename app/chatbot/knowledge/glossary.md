## Stop-loss
A stop-loss closes a trade automatically when the price moves against you by an amount you chose in advance. Example: you buy at ₹100 and set a stop-loss at ₹98. If the price falls to ₹98 the trade closes, so the loss is about ₹2 per share. In fast markets the exit price can be slightly worse than ₹98 (see slippage).

## Position size
Position size is how many shares, or how much money, you put into one trade. Example: 50 shares at ₹200 is a ₹10,000 position. A bigger position makes every price move matter more.

## Risk per trade
Risk per trade is the most you are willing to lose on a single trade, usually as a percentage of your account. Example: 1% of ₹1,00,000 is ₹1,000. If your stop-loss is ₹2 below your entry, a ₹1,000 risk allows a position of 500 shares.

## Drawdown
Drawdown is the fall from your account's highest value to a later low. Example: the account peaks at ₹1,10,000 and later drops to ₹99,000. The drawdown is ₹11,000, which is 10%.

## Win rate
Win rate is the share of closed trades that made money. Example: 6 winning trades out of 10 is a 60% win rate. Win rate alone says nothing about profit, because the size of wins and losses also matters.

## Average win, average loss and payoff ratio
Average win is the typical profit on winning trades. Average loss is the typical loss on losing trades. The payoff ratio is average win divided by average loss. Example: an average win of ₹300 and an average loss of ₹900 give a payoff ratio of 0.33.

## Expectancy
Expectancy is the average result per trade. Formula: win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss. Example: 0.6 × 300 − 0.4 × 900 = −₹180 per trade, so despite a 60% win rate this pattern loses money.

## Break-even win rate
The break-even win rate is the win rate you need just to cover your losses. Formula: average loss ÷ (average win + average loss). Example: 900 ÷ (300 + 900) = 75%.

## Realised and unrealised P&L
Realised P&L comes from trades you have closed. Unrealised P&L is the paper profit or loss on positions still open, at the current price. Unrealised P&L can change or disappear before you close the trade.

## Candlestick
A candlestick summarises price in one time period with four values: open, high, low and close. The body shows the move between open and close, and the thin lines show the high and low.

## Volatility
Volatility is how much and how fast a price moves. Higher volatility means bigger swings in both directions, so the same position size carries more risk.

## Slippage
Slippage is the gap between the price you expected and the price your order actually got. Example: you expect to buy at ₹100 but fill at ₹100.30, so slippage is ₹0.30 per share.

## Leverage and margin
Leverage means trading a bigger position than your own money alone would allow, using borrowed funds. It multiplies gains and losses by the same factor, so a small move against you can cause a large loss.

## Overtrading
Overtrading is trading much more often, or with much bigger size, than your own plan allows. Each trade has costs, and each decision adds a chance of an emotional mistake.

## Paper trading
Paper trading means practising with virtual money. No real orders are placed. It is for learning habits, and results do not predict how you will behave with real money.

## Risk-reward ratio
Risk-reward compares what you stand to gain with what you risk on a trade. Example: risking ₹500 to try to make ₹1,000 is a 1:2 risk-reward ratio. A high ratio only helps if the target is realistic.
